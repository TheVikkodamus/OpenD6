## 1. Introduction

The **OpenD6 Space** system is a fast, cinematic roleplaying framework built entirely on six-sided dice. Every number representing a character's ability is expressed as a **die code** — a number of d6s (plus optional bonus pips) rolled and summed against a **difficulty number** set by the Game Master (GM).

### The Game in a Nutshell

Roll some six-sided dice equal to your character's relevant skill or attribute die code.  
Add them up, including any bonus pips (+1 or +2).  
Compare the total to the difficulty number.  
**Equal to or higher = success.**

That is the entire resolution system. Everything else in this document is refinement.

## 2. Core Mechanics

### 2.1 Die Codes

A die code is written as **XD+Y**, where:

- **X** = the number of six-sided dice you roll.
- **+Y** = pips added to the total after rolling (0, +1, or +2 only — three pips equals one additional die).

**Progression:** +1, +2, 1D, 1D+1, 1D+2, 2D, 2D+1, 2D+2, 3D, 3D+1 … and so on.

> *Example: A die code of 3D+1 means roll three d6s, add them together, then add 1 to the result.*

### 2.2 The Wild Die

Whenever you make **any** skill, attribute, or damage roll, designate one die as the **Wild Die** (use a different color). The Wild Die introduces cinematic swings of fortune.

| Wild Die Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **6** | **Critical Success** — Add the 6, then roll the Wild Die again and add that too. Keep rolling as long as 6s appear. |
| **2–5** | Normal — Add the result as usual. |
| **1** | **Critical Failure** — The highest non-wild die is cancelled. Add the remaining dice normally, but a complication occurs (GM decides severity based on the remaining total). |

> **Note:** After the initial roll, rolling a 1 on a re-rolled Wild Die during a Critical Success chain does *not* cause a Critical Failure — only the first 1 matters.

### 2.3 Difficulty Numbers

| Difficulty Level | Number Range |
|---|---|
| Very Easy | 1–5 |
| Easy | 6–10 |
| Moderate | 11–15 |
| Difficult | 16–20 |
| Very Difficult | 21–25 |
| Heroic | 26–30 |
| Heroic+ | 31+ |

The GM sets difficulties based on the situation. They may also tell the player the *label* (e.g., "Difficult") without revealing the exact number. The player may then decide whether to spend Character Points or Fate Points before rolling.

### 2.4 Opposed Rolls

When two characters directly contest each other, both roll their relevant skill or attribute. The higher total wins. On a tie, the initiating character succeeds.

### 2.5 Taking It Slowly

A character who takes extra time on a task earns bonus dice. For every additional time period equal to the normal task duration, the character gains **+1D** to the roll. The GM may limit or disallow this in time-critical situations.

### 2.6 Multiple Actions

A character may attempt more than one action in a single round. For each action beyond the first, subtract **1D** from **all** skill or attribute rolls made that round (including the first action).

> *Example: A pilot who wants to fire a weapon and perform a maneuver in the same round subtracts 1D from both rolls.*

### 2.7 Spending Character Points

After making a roll but **before** the GM declares the result, you may spend **Character Points** to add bonus dice. Each Character Point spent adds one additional die. You may spend a maximum of **2 Character Points** on any single roll.

### 2.8 Spending Fate Points

**Before** making a roll, you may spend a **Fate Point** to **double** your die code for that one roll (pips are also doubled; all modifiers apply after doubling). Only one Fate Point may be spent per roll, and only one die still counts as the Wild Die.

## 3. Attributes

Attributes are broad aptitudes that underpin all skills. Each starts at a base value established during character creation. Skills built on an attribute always use at least the attribute's die code if the character has no specific skill training.

### 3.1 Core Attributes (Space Setting)

| Attribute | Alternate Names | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordination** | Aim, Dexterity | Manual dexterity, ranged combat, fine motor skills, picking locks, thrown weapons. |
| **Endurance** | Constitution, Stamina | Bodily resistance to physical damage, fatigue, poison, disease. |
| **Reflexes** | Agility, Balance | Reaction time, gross motor coordination, dodging, melee fighting, piloting under pressure. |
| **Strength** | Athletics, Physique | Raw physical power, lifting, melee damage, climbing under load. |
| **Knowledge** | Lore, Wisdom, Science | Education, academic knowledge, history, languages, law, cultural awareness. |
| **Mechanical** | Technology, Mechanics | Operating and repairing vehicles, starships, shields, sensors, and mechanical devices. |
| **Perception** | Awareness, Cognition | Observation, social interaction, reading people, bargaining, searching. |
| **Technical** | Technology | Repairing and modifying equipment, computers, cybernetics, demolitions, electronic security. |

> **Note on attribute selection:** Not all attributes need to be used in every campaign. A straight action-adventure setting might need only Coordination, Endurance, Reflexes, Strength, Knowledge, Mechanical, and Perception. Add or remove attributes to suit your setting.

### 3.2 Starting Attribute Dice

Characters begin with a number of attribute dice equal to **3D per attribute** in use. For a standard 6-attribute space game that gives **18D** to distribute. Typical range per attribute: 2D minimum, 4D maximum (3D average). Special species or circumstances may alter these limits.

## 4. Skills

Skills are specific applications of an attribute. The skill's die code is **added to** the governing attribute to form the full roll. A character without a specific skill rolls the bare attribute instead.

> *Example: A character with Coordination 3D and no Ranged training rolls 3D when firing. A character with Coordination 3D and Ranged +2 rolls 3D+2.*

### 4.2 Advanced Skills

Skills marked **advanced** require a prerequisite base skill at a minimum of **+6** before they can be learned. Learning an advanced skill costs double the normal amount.

### 4.3 Specializations

A character may specialize in a narrow application of a skill (e.g., *Ranged: Sniper Rifles* or *Starship Piloting: Alliance Frigates*). A specialization costs **5 Character Points or 1D of starting skill dice**. When the specific application applies, the character rolls the specialization die code rather than the base skill. The base skill does not improve when the specialization improves.

> *Example: A character with Ranged 3D+6 and a specialization Ranged: Shotguns +3 rolls 3D+9 when firing shotguns, and 3D+6 for all other ranged weapons.*

Maximum one specialization per skill. Maximum starting value for a specialization: +9 skill points.

## 5. Character Creation

### 5.1 Step-by-Step Summary

1. **Concept** — Choose species, name, background, and general role.
2. **Choose Attributes** — Agree with the GM on which attributes are in use for this setting.
3. **Distribute Attribute Dice** — Split the starting pool (3D × number of attributes) across all attributes. Minimum per attribute: 2D. Maximum: 4D (exceptions by species).
4. **Choose Trained Skills** — Select a number of trained skills per attribute equal to the die code of that attribute plus the die code of Knowledge (in pips). Example: Perception 3D+1 and Knowledge 2D+2 = 6D+3, meaning you may choose 6 trained Perception skills (ignoring leftover pips for this count — round down).
5. **Distribute Skill Dice** — The starting pool is **7D** of skill points. Add these to trained skills only. Maximum added to any single skill at creation: **+6** (or +9 for specializations). Points are added as pips (1 point = +1 pip; 3 pips = +1D).
6. **Derived Statistics** — Calculate derived stats (see Section 5.2).
7. **Advantages & Disadvantages** — Select any desired advantages and disadvantages (see Section 5.3). Net die-code result adjusts your starting skill dice pool (positive = extra skill points; negative = fewer).
8. **Character Points & Fate Points** — Starting characters begin with **5 Character Points** and **1 Fate Point** (GM may adjust).
9. **Starting Credits** — Determined by the GM based on the setting. A common default: 3D × 250 credits, modified by Knowledge die code and relevant skills.
10. **Purchase Equipment** — Spend starting credits on gear.
11. **Background & Personality** — Note personality traits, history, goals, and quirks.

### 5.2 Derived Statistics

| Statistic | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Hit Points** | (Strength dice × 3) + 20 + any species bonus. Add pips at face value. |
| **Damage Resistance (DR)** | Strength die code (number before D) + armor DR bonus. |
| **Ranged Defense Value (RDV)** | (Coordination dice × 3) + pips + Dodge skill points. |
| **Melee Defense Value (MDV)** | (Strength dice × 3) + pips + Melee skill points. |
| **Brawling Defense Value (BDV)** | (Strength dice × 3) + pips + Brawling skill points. |
| **Starship Defense Value** | (Mechanical dice × 3) + pips + Starship Piloting skill points. |
| **Initiative** | (Perception × 3) + pips + (1 per 1D in Coordination) + (1 per 6 Search points) + (1 per 6 Tactics points). |
| **Move** | 10 meters per round (standard humanoid). Spend 1D of attribute dice at creation to gain +1 Move. |

## 6. Character Advancement

### 6.1 Earning Character Points

The GM awards Character Points at the end of adventures based on performance. General guidelines per adventure (average 2-session adventure):

| Achievement | Character Points |
|---|---|
| Participated (baseline) | 3–4 |
| Accomplished the main goal | +1 |
| Clever problem solving | +2 |
| Good teamwork | +2 |
| Excellent individual roleplaying | +2 |
| Everyone had fun | +1–3 |
| Completion of particularly long or hard adventure | GM increases all above |

Fate Points (1–2) are awarded when the group achieves critical goals or a player goes above and beyond in memorable fashion.

### 6.2 Improving Skills

Spend Character Points between adventures.

**Learning a new skill:**
> Cost = 5 − (number before D in governing attribute). Minimum cost: 1.
> New skill starts at attribute die code + 1 pip.

**Improving an existing trained skill by +1 pip:**
> Cost = the number of skill points the skill will *have after* improvement.
> Example: Raising Ranged from +5 to +6 costs 6 Character Points.

**Improving a specialization by +1:**
> Cost = half the number of skill points the specialization will have after improvement (round up).

> Skills and specializations may each only improve by 1 point between adventures.

### 6.3 Improving Attributes

Attributes represent innate potential and are harder to raise. Improving an attribute by 1 pip costs **10 × (number before D in that attribute) Character Points**. Generally only one attribute may improve by one pip per adventure, and each improvement requires a roll to confirm (see OpenD6 core rules for advancement roll details).

### 6.4 Advancing Talents / Powers

If the setting uses special powers (psionics, biotics, etc.), advancement follows the same pip-cost formula as skills. Tech and Combat Talents are skill-based; Power/Biotic Talents advance by pip at the skill-point cost rate.

### 6.5 Veteran Points

Veteran Points equal the cumulative Character Points a character has ever earned (not spent — always accumulating). They have no mechanical effect; the GM uses them to gauge relative character experience and calibrate adventure difficulty.

## 7. Species Templates

> **Format:** Attribute ranges are listed as Minimum/Maximum. If only one value appears, it is fixed.

## 8. Combat

### 8.1 Combat Sequence

At the start of each combat encounter, all combatants roll for **Initiative**. The character with the highest result acts first, then the next highest, and so on. Ties go to the character with the higher Perception attribute.

**Initiative = (Perception × 3) + pips + applicable modifiers**

Optionally, use the **Simultaneous** method: all combatants on one side act, then the other side. Roll 1D; the side with the higher result goes first.

### 8.2 Declaring Actions

Before rolling, declare how many actions you are taking this round. Subtract 1D from **all** rolls for each action beyond the first (before rolling). Minimum die code after penalties is 1D (always roll at least one die).

A **Full Reaction** (spending the entire round doing nothing but defending) adds +10 to your appropriate Defense Value but prevents any other action.

### 8.3 Making an Attack

1. Roll your relevant combat skill (or attribute if untrained).
2. Compare the total to the target's **Defense Value**.
3. If your roll **equals or exceeds** the Defense Value: **hit** — roll damage.
4. If the roll is lower: **miss**.

**Base Defense Values (unarmed, unarmored target):**
- Ranged Defense Value (RDV) = (Coordination dice × 3) + pips + Dodge points
- Melee Defense Value (MDV) = (Strength dice × 3) + pips + Melee points
- Brawling Defense Value (BDV) = (Strength dice × 3) + pips + Brawling points

### 8.4 Attack Modifiers

| Situation | Modifier to Attack Roll |
|---|---|
| Point-blank range | +1D |
| Short range | 0 |
| Medium range | −1D |
| Long range | −2D |
| Aim (spend one action, +1D next shot) | +1D |
| Called shot (target specific location) | −2D |
| Blinded or attacking in complete darkness | −2D |
| Target behind 25% cover | 0 |
| Target behind 50% cover | −1D |
| Target behind 75% cover | −2D |

### 8.5 Defense Options

**Dodge:** Roll your Dodge skill; the result replaces 10 as the attack difficulty. Dodging works against any attack type. Costs one action.

**Parry:** Roll your relevant melee skill (Brawling, Melee, or weapon skill). If your result exceeds the attacker's roll, the attack is blocked. Only usable against melee attacks. Costs one action.

**Full Reaction:** +10 to appropriate defense; no other actions this round.

### 8.6 Weapon Overheating (Ranged Weapons)

When a ranged weapon roll produces a **Critical Failure** (Wild Die = 1 on the initial roll), roll the Wild Die again:

- **1, 2, or 3:** The weapon **overheats** and cannot be fired for 2 rounds.
- **4, 5, or 6:** Normal miss.

### 8.7 Called Shots & Special Attacks

**Head Shot:** Attack difficulty +12. Half the damage dice become Wild Dice. Can kill or severely incapacitate.

**Targeted Shot (limb/arm/leg):** Attack difficulty +12. Damage is normal, but the target cannot be killed by this shot. On a leg hit: target is knocked prone and must spend an action standing. On an arm hit: target drops whatever is in that hand.

**Charge:** Attacker rushes at target. On a hit with a melee weapon, add +1D to damage.

**Disarm:** Treat as a called shot (−2D to attack). On success, the target drops a held object.

**Grapple/Pin:** Both combatants make Strength rolls. The winner controls the grapple; the loser can only attempt to break free each round.

### 8.8 Kinetic Barriers (Shields)

Characters and vehicles in advanced settings often carry **Kinetic Barriers (KB)** — additional protection against ranged and explosive attacks. KB points are subtracted from incoming damage **before** DR is applied.

- KB does **not** apply against melee/brawling attacks, poison, temperature extremes, or radiation.
- Once KB reaches 0, it fails.
- Armor KB regenerates at **3 points per round** if the character takes **no damage for 2 consecutive rounds**.
- Ship/vehicle KB regenerates differently (see Section 12).

### 8.9 Explosives & Blast Radius

Explosive weapons list a **blast radius** as a series of values (e.g., 2/4/6 meters).

- Characters within the **innermost** radius suffer **full damage**.
- Characters in the **second** range suffer **half** damage.
- Characters in the **outermost** range suffer **one-quarter** damage.
- Characters beyond the final range suffer no damage.

## 9. Damage & Healing

### 9.1 Resolving Damage

After a successful hit:

1. **Attacker rolls weapon damage.**
2. **Defender rolls Endurance** (or adds DR from species, armor, and shields).
3. **Defender subtracts their total (DR + KB) from the attacker's damage roll.**
4. The net result = **damage taken in Hit Points (HP)**.
5. Consult the wound table.

> Kinetic Barriers are consumed first. Each KB point absorbs 1 point of incoming ranged/explosive damage. Remaining damage is then reduced by DR.

### 9.2 Hit Points & Wound Levels

| Hit Points Remaining | Wound Level | Penalty to All Rolls |
|---|---|---|
| 100%–66% | Lightly Wounded | −3 |
| 65%–33% | Wounded | −9 |
| 32%–1% | Severely Wounded | −15 |
| 0 | Almost Dead | Cannot act |
| Below 0 | Dead | — |

> Wound penalties do **not** apply to Endurance rolls made to resist further damage.

### 9.3 Armor

Armor adds its **DR** value to the character's innate DR when resisting damage. It also contributes its **KB** to the kinetic barrier pool. Some armors impose penalties to Dexterity/Strength-based actions or to Biotic/Technical talent use.

### 9.4 Natural Healing

Characters heal naturally during rest. Roll Strength + modifiers for HP recovered at the start of each rest period:

| Strength Roll | HP Recovered |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1–5 | 2 |
| 6–10 | 3 |
| 11–15 | 6 |
| 16–20 | 9 |
| 21–25 | 12 |
| 26–30 | 15 |
| 31+ | 18 |

**Rest Period by Wound Level:**

| Wound Level | Rest Period Before Healing Roll |
|---|---|
| Lightly Wounded | 1 hour |
| Wounded | 1 day |
| Severely Wounded | 1 week |

Add **+12** to the Strength roll for a full day of complete rest (sedentary activities only). **0** modifier for light activity. **−3** for a day spent fighting.

### 9.5 Medical Treatment

A character with First Aid or Medicine may attempt to heal a wounded companion **once per day**.

| Victim's Current Wound Level | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Lightly Wounded (75–100% HP) | Very Easy (5) |
| Wounded (50–74% HP) | Easy (10) |
| Severely Wounded (25–50% HP) | Moderate (15) |
| Almost Dead (1–25% HP) | Difficult (20) |
| 0 HP | Very Difficult (25) |

Success = victim heals 1 wound level (using the Wound system) or 2D HP (using the Hit Point system).

Medicine (advanced skill) handles surgery and recovery from critical injuries. The GM sets higher difficulties for complex procedures.

### 9.6 Character Death

If medical aid (First Aid with a Very Easy difficulty check) is not provided within **10 seconds** (2 combat rounds) of a character reaching 0 HP, the character dies and **cannot be revived** by conventional means.

### 11.2 Scale and Damage

Objects of different scales interact differently:

1. Find the **difference** in scale die codes between the two objects (e.g., character scale 0 vs. tank scale 4D = difference of 4D).
2. The **smaller** object gains that die code as a **bonus to hit** the larger object.
3. The **larger** object **loses** that die code from its attack roll against the smaller object (hitting a charging soldier from a capital ship gun is extremely difficult).
4. The **larger** object's weapons gain that die code as a **damage bonus** against the smaller target.

## 12. Space Travel & Combat

### 12.1 FTL Travel

Most large starships use a **mass effect drive core** (or equivalent FTL technology). Key mechanics:

**FTL Charge Saturation Time:** The time needed to fully charge the drive before a jump. A ship must charge before entering FTL.

**FTL Discharge Time:** After exiting FTL, the drive core must vent heat. The ship cannot re-enter FTL or operate at peak efficiency during this window. Discharging in orbit of a gas giant or star is faster; discharging in deep space takes the full stated time.

**Calculating Transit Time:** Distance is measured relative to the GM's star-map. A standard FTL Speed of 1 covers a short interplanetary distance per day; higher Speed ratings cover proportionally greater distances. The GM sets transit times appropriate to the setting's scale.

### 12.2 Space Sensors

Sensor values are given as **Active / Passive** (combat range units).

- **Active sensors** emit signals, are detectable, and have the listed range.
- **Passive sensors** listen only and are not detectable, but have longer range (typically 2–4× active range).

Detecting stealthy ships, emissions-controlled vessels, or objects in sensor-degrading environments raises difficulty. Bare minimum detection of a ship with active stealth may require a Heroic (30+) Sensors roll.

### 12.3 Space Combat Scale

Space combat uses the same mechanics as personal combat but at **ship scale**. Key differences:

- All piloting checks use **Starship Piloting** or **Capital Ship Piloting**.
- All weapons use **Starship Gunnery** or **Capital Ship Gunnery**.
- Defense = Pilot's **Starship Piloting + Maneuverability** die code (pilot may also Full Dodge for +10 to Defense).
- Damage and HP are at ship scale; scale modifiers apply when capital ships fire on fighters or characters (see Section 11.2).

**Ship Initiative:** Roll Mechanical attribute for the ship's pilot; add +1 per point of the ship's Speed rating above the slowest ship in the engagement.

### 12.4 GARDIAN Anti-Fighter Systems

GARDIAN (General Array Defense Integration and Neutralization) systems are point-defense laser arrays on capital ships. They use the **Sensors (active)** skill and cover all arcs simultaneously. Primarily effective against fighters, missiles, and small craft (character scale). Against capital-scale ships, they have minimal effect.

### 12.5 Disruptor Torpedoes & Missiles

Torpedoes and missiles are guided; they follow targets and can track through evasion. To intercept:
- The target's pilot may attempt to outmaneuver (Starship Piloting vs. a difficulty set by the missile's guidance rating).
- A GARDIAN system may attempt to intercept before impact.
- On a hit, torpedoes bypass **all Kinetic Barriers** and deal full damage to the hull.

### 12.6 Capital Ship Combat Actions

Per combat round, a capital ship's crew may:
- **Pilot:** Maneuver/dodge (Starship/Capital Ship Piloting).
- **Fire Weapons:** Each weapon battery requires a crewmember and the appropriate gunnery skill.
- **Operate Shields:** Direct shield power, regenerate KB, or raise/lower shields for atmospheric entry.
- **Sensors:** Scan for enemies, lock on, or attempt to jam enemy targeting.
- **Engineering:** Conduct emergency repairs, manage heat (discharge issues), or redirect power.

Multi-action penalties apply per station: a ship with a skeleton crew suffers penalties as crew attempt multiple functions.

### 12.7 Atmospheric Entry

Ships with Kinetic Barriers must **lower their shields** before atmospheric entry. A standard entry requires no piloting roll. Combat entry (at speed, under fire, non-ideal angle) requires a Moderate to Difficult Piloting roll depending on conditions. Capital-class vessels generally **cannot** safely enter planetary atmospheres.

## 14. Game Mastering

### 14.1 The GM's Role

The GM serves as **Director** (setting scenes), **Character Actor** (playing all non-player characters), **Referee** (deciding what's possible and setting difficulties), **World Creator** (building the setting), and optionally **Game Designer** (adapting or creating rules modules). You need not fill all roles at once; even running pre-written adventures makes you a Director and Referee.

### 14.2 Setting Difficulties

Don't over-think it. Use the difficulty table, pick a label that feels right, choose a number in that range, and move on. The OpenD6 system is designed to run fast. Trust your instincts.

- **Very Easy (1–5):** Almost anyone can do it.
- **Easy (6–10):** A competent character rarely fails.
- **Moderate (11–15):** Requires skill or training.
- **Difficult (16–20):** Even trained characters sweat.
- **Very Difficult (21–25):** Experts pushed to their limits.
- **Heroic (26–30):** Only the best, in perfect conditions.
- **Heroic+ (31+):** Near-legendary feats.

### 14.3 Awarding Character Points

See Section 6.1. Award regularly, be consistent, and err on the generous side for good roleplaying and creative problem-solving.

### 14.4 Adventure Structure

A typical adventure consists of linked **scenes** (location- or time-triggered encounters):

- **Setup:** Characters learn their goal.
- **Investigation / Information Gathering:** Clues point toward the objective.
- **Roleplaying / Negotiation:** Social encounters with GMCs.
- **Thrill Scenes:** Tense, time-limited challenges (use the real-time dice mechanic: players have 10–15 seconds to roll a target number of sixes).
- **Chase:** A pursuit sequence with listed obstacles by round or location.
- **Combat:** Characters must fight through opposition.
- **Climax:** The final confrontation or challenge that resolves the main plot.

### 14.5 Fudging the Dice

The GM may secretly adjust die rolls or difficulty numbers at critical moments to keep the story exciting. Do this sparingly, always behind a screen, and always fairly — sometimes in the players' favor, sometimes against them.

### 14.6 NPC Difficulty Guidelines

Use the skill die codes below to quickly assign appropriate skill totals:

| Die Code | Description |
|---|---|
| 1D | Below average humanoid |
| 2D | Untrained average humanoid |
| 3D | Average trained person |
| 4D | Professional level |
| 5D | Above-average expert |
| 6D | Best in a city (roughly 1 in 100,000) |
| 7D | Best on a continent |
| 8D | Best on a world |
| 9D | Best in a star system |
| 10D+ | Galactic legend |

### 14.7 Scaling Adventures by Experience

Add up all player characters' **Veteran Points** (total Character Points ever earned) to gauge the group's combined experience. Increase enemy skill die codes, HP, and number of opponents proportionally as the group's total grows.
